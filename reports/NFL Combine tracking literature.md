# Sprint Mechanics Bridge Combine Drills and Rookie Games

The literature offers a 2027 Big Data Bowl writeup three main things. First, a **validated physical model**: the mono-exponential speed curve and the force-velocity profile (Samozino 2016, Clark 2019, Morin 2021). Fitted to the 40-yard dash, it summarises a Combine drill as capacity parameters (vmax, τ, A0, S0), and the same parameters can be estimated again from a rookie's game frames. Second, a **deceleration and change-of-direction (COD) vocabulary**: the COD deficit, the angle-velocity trade-off, and braking thresholds. These turn drill times and route breaks into phases you can interpret. Third, a **statistical playbook** for about 500 players spread over 15 positions: reliability first, empirical-Bayes shrinkage, latent-variable incremental validity, Freedman–Lane permutation, FDR control, and selection models. The opening is real. Peer-reviewed Combine validity work rests almost entirely on stopwatch times and box-score outcomes, and the findings are weak or mixed outside running-back sprint measures ([Kuzmits & Adams 2008](https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn/openlb/publication/OLB-PM-18841077); [Vincent et al. 2019](https://digitalcommons.uri.edu/kinesiology_facpubs/89)). The research notes found **no peer-reviewed study** that fits sprint models to Combine *tracking*, builds in-game acceleration-speed profiles in the NFL, or links Combine RFID data to in-game Next Gen Stats. The public repo "Measure the Athlete, Not the Drill" already covers the most obvious angle: Combine versus in-game COD stability, judged by split-half reliability and family-wise permutation ([bansal1600](https://github.com/bansal1600/kaggle-NFL-big-data-2027)). The five ideas proposed below avoid it. They model **capacity utilisation, drill-phase decomposition, motion-model ghosts, pass-rush get-off physics and market-adjusted selection**, not cross-context COD stability. One caveat applies throughout: no full texts could be opened while the notes were compiled. Numbers come from abstracts and record pages, and each reference below carries a verification status.

## A two-parameter sprint model turns the 40 into physics

The model behind most of this work is v(t) = vmax·(1 − e^(−t/τ)). Samozino and colleagues turned it into a field method that estimates step-averaged horizontal force, power and mechanical effectiveness "from only anthropometric and spatiotemporal data". Against force plates in 9 sprinters it showed low bias and **between-trial SEM under 5%** ([UPO record](https://investiga.upo.es/documentos/5ec78fe72999520a1d5580cc)). Clark et al. applied it to public 2016 Combine splits. They found that **maximum velocity explained r² = 0.72, 0.83 and 0.94** of 9.1 m, 18.3 m and 36.6 m performance, and that fast and slow groups shared a similar τ of about 0.77 s ([LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4054398?lng=de)). A 2025 JSCR paper scaled this to **2,156 Combine players**: DBs and WRs had the highest vmax and amax and the lowest τ, and OL the reverse ([LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4089847?lng=en)). Both studies fit the curve to only 2–3 split times. A 4.4 s dash recorded at 10 Hz yields about 44 samples, so tracking should estimate τ more tightly. Two cautions: vendor smoothing of the speed field may bias τ upward, and onset detection needs a time-shift parameter. The {shorts} package handles the second for timing gates ([LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/dlv/Record/4076968); [CRAN](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/shorts/readme/README.html)).

The in-game counterpart is Morin et al.'s **in-situ acceleration-speed (A–S) profile**. For each speed bin, take the maximal accelerations observed in routine GPS data, then fit a line. Profiles were "highly linear (all R² > 0.984)" and reliable two weeks apart ([LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4070585?lng=en)). A reliability follow-up in U19 players warned that the data must span **20–95% of maximum speed**, and that this spread matters more than the number of sessions ([LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/dfb/Record/4081845?lng=en)). Rugby union work added density-based outlier removal before fitting ([PMC10784250](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10784250/)). On curved paths, A–S area and S0 fall as the radius tightens ([Biology of Sport](https://www.termedia.pl/Does-curvilinear-sprint-training-with-different-radii-improve-r-nmultidirectional-speed-and-sprint-mechanics-in-youth-soccer-r-nplayers-,78,57300,0,0.html)). This matters because about **85% of maximum-velocity actions in elite team sports are curvilinear** ([PMC7179881](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7179881)). Together these give a *common currency*: the same A0 and S0 can be estimated from the Combine 40 and from a rookie's season of snaps. The notes found no A–S study in American football. A practitioner critique sharpens the stakes: "football players rarely run a 40-yard dash during competition nor do they reach maximal velocity" ([1080 Motion](https://1080motion.com/vmax-short-sprint-performance/)). For a writeup, the question that follows is *how much of the Combine capacity a rookie actually expresses on Sundays*.

## Braking, not turning, explains much of an agility time

Total COD time is confounded by linear speed. Nimphius et al. defined the **COD deficit** (505 time minus 10 m sprint time). It was essentially unrelated to sprint time, and it re-ranked 5 of 17 athletes ([ECU repository](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworkspost2013/2635)). Lockie et al. adapted the test to American football (the A505). It showed ICC 0.84–0.95, and R/DB were faster than linemen in it ([PubMed 27227795](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27227795/)). Sheppard and Young's classification matters for interpretation. The 5-10-5 and 3-cone are pre-planned, so they measure *COD speed*, not agility, which requires responding to a stimulus ([BISp](https://www.bisp-surf.de/Record/PU201401000015)). Dos'Santos et al. formalised the **angle-velocity trade-off**: "faster approaches compromise the execution of the intended COD", and sharper, faster cuts raise knee load ([Salford repository](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1378901/the-effect-of-angle-and-velocity-on-change-of-direction-biomechanics-an-angle-velocity-trade-off)). Harper's reviews provide numbers for the braking phase. Peak deceleration forces are **up to 2.7 times** those of the first steps of maximal acceleration ([PMC9474351](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9474351/)). The match-play meta-analysis uses **>2.5 m·s⁻² (high) and >3.5 m·s⁻² (very high)** as event thresholds ([PMC6851047](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6851047/)).

Reliability is the warning. The pro-agility test is reliable as a whole, with a typical error of about 2%, though the primary source is unverified ([Physiopedia](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Pro-Agility_%285-10-5%29_Test)). An AUT thesis split the shuttle into phases and found that timing-light phases were reliable (ICC 0.90–0.99), while **radar-derived phase velocities were not (ICC 0.14–0.76)** ([AUT repository](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/items/7fa1f785-f30c-4202-ad37-eae757cf0a6c)). This fits the public 2027 finding that Combine COD is unstable while in-game COD is stable, at split-half 0.63–0.95 ([bansal1600](https://github.com/bansal1600/kaggle-NFL-big-data-2027)). The implication is that phase-level metrics from 10 Hz drill tracking must be audited before use. Event-level summaries (braking onset speed, braking distance, minimum speed at the turn, exit τ) are likely more reliable than instantaneous velocities. The notes found no primary reliability study for the 3-cone, and no peer-reviewed tracking decomposition of Combine drills. The 2019 Big Data Bowl paper that led to the Figure-8 drill is the only league precedent, and its authors and metric are unverified ([NFL Operations](https://operations.nfl.com/gameday/analytics/stats-articles/big-data-bowl-winning-paper-leads-to-new-drill-at-the-nfl-combine)).

## Forty years of Combine studies predict the draft better than the game

Two strands of research should not be conflated. Combine tests predict *draft status* reasonably well for speed positions. The 3-cone separated drafted from undrafted players in skill, big-skill and linemen groups alike ([Sierer et al. 2008](https://busqueda.bvsalud.org/portal/resource/es/mdl-18296949)). For *NFL performance*, results are thin. Kuzmits and Adams found "no consistent statistical relationship" except sprint tests for RBs ([PMID 18841077](https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn/openlb/publication/OLB-PM-18841077)). Teramoto et al. found the **10-yd split** to be the best predictor of RB yards per carry, and height and vertical jump the best for WR yards per reception ([NASE summary](https://www.naseinc.com/blog/predictors-of-performance-in-pro-football/)). Vincent et al. found at least one significant drill at every position, with samples as small as **44 QBs and 59 DEs**, and called the tests "modest" predictors ([URI](https://digitalcommons.uri.edu/kinesiology_facpubs/89)). Robbins found that normalising for body size adds no predictive value for draft order ([BISp](https://www.bisp-surf.de/Record/PU201104003890)). For QBs, Wolfson et al. concluded that college and Combine statistics add little, and that teams already combine pre-draft information well ([UMN](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/the-quarterback-prediction-problem-forecasting-the-performance-of/)). The 10-yd split result is notable. It is the Combine stopwatch number closest to τ and amax, which tracking can now estimate directly.

The outcomes these studies use are contaminated. Keefer's regression discontinuity at the round 1/2 boundary found that **a 10% increase in salary-cap value buys about 2.7 additional starts**, with no productivity difference ([MetaTOC](https://metatoc.com/papers/63649-the-sunk-cost-fallacy-in-the-national-football-league-salary-cap-value-and-playing-time)). Massey and Thaler showed that top picks are overvalued relative to the surplus they deliver ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v59y2013i7p1479-1495.html)). Games played, starts and cumulative yards therefore partly measure draft capital. Rate outcomes (per-snap, per-route, the NGS play-level stats) and explicit selection modelling are not optional. The notes found **no Combine validity paper that applies a draft-capital discontinuity or a selection correction**, nor any analysis of drill opt-outs. NGS's own Draft Model and 2025 "Combine IQ" use RFID drill tracking. They publish no validation statistics, and their formulas are proprietary ([NFL.com](https://www.nfl.com/news/next-gen-stats-draft-model-can-predict-prospects-pro-success-0ap3000001103347); [AP via ClickOnDetroit](https://www.clickondetroit.com/tech/2025/02/27/nfl-nextgen-stats-team-unveils-new-tool-to-help-fans-better-understand-combine-results/)).

## Winning Big Data Bowl entries define events, ghosts and shrunk player effects

Most landmark football tracking models need all 22 players: Going Deep, EHCP, DeepQB and the 2020 "Zoo" CNN ([Yurko et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.01760); [Deshpande & Evans 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12337); [NFL Ops 2020](https://operations.nfl.com/stats-central/stats-articles/2020-big-data-bowl-recap)). They cannot be computed on 2027 data, which reportedly tracks only the rookie. Several methods do transfer to a single player's track. Chu et al.'s route clustering uses only the receiver's own path ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.02423)), so kinematic features can be conditioned on route type. STRAIN, a closing rate divided by distance and estimated with a multilevel model, can be approximated against a fixed pocket point ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10262)). Brefeld et al.'s **individual probabilistic movement models** estimate a player's displacement distribution over a time lag, conditioned on current velocity, from that player's own data ([author PDF](https://ml3.leuphana.de/publications/mlj-soccer2018.pdf)). A Vienna validation paper warns that constant-acceleration motion models without a speed cap misclassify reachable regions ([univie](https://eprints.cs.univie.ac.at/7324)). A sprint-model-based cap is exactly the fix that paper calls for.

Winning entries follow a recognisable template. They define a frame-level event or probability, aggregate it per player, and shrink it. The 2024 winner charged a missed tackle when **tackle probability exceeded 75% for at least 0.5 s** and then collapsed ([Knowable](https://knowablemagazine.org/content/article/technology/2024/how-next-gen-data-analytics-changing-american-football)). The 2026 winner, "Ghostbusters", judged defenders against hypothetical ghost distributions ([NFL Ops](https://operations.nfl.com/programs-initiatives/innovation/big-data-bowl)). The fractional-tackles finalist used momentum ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.14769)). A 2025 CMU poster estimated a maximal acceleration-speed frontier for 69 RBs as an effort proxy and found that **effort alone did not explain play success** ([CMU SURE](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/cmsac/sure/2025/showcase/football_effort/poster.pdf)). That is a useful negative prior: raw frontier position needs context and a capacity reference.

## Small samples demand reliability audits before any validity claim

With about 33 players per position, three statistical results set the design. First, reliability caps validity. Spearman's correction r_true = r_obs/√(rel_X·rel_Y) is the reason to report reliabilities next to every coefficient ([gwern reprint](https://www.gwern.net/doc/psychology/1904-spearman.pdf)). Frost and Thompson show that the correction factor is itself imprecise when the subset with repeated measurements is small ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jorssa/v163y2000i2p173-189.html)). Second, Westfall and Yarkoni showed that regression-based incremental-validity claims have Type I error rates that **approach 100%** when the control covariate is unreliable ([PMC4816570](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4816570/)). "Tracking metric X predicts beyond the 40" is exactly that situation, and it calls for a latent speed factor estimated with SEM, or for SIMEX ([Carroll et al. 2006](https://www.routledge.com/Measurement-Error-in-Nonlinear-Models-A-Modern-Perspective/Carroll-Ruppert-Stefanski-Crainiceanu/p/book/9781584886334)). Third, shrinkage pays. Efron and Morris report that empirical-Bayes rules achieve **under half the mean squared error** of raw means ([JASA PDF](https://www.medicine.mcgill.ca/epidemiology/hanley/bios602/MultilevelData/EfronMorrisJASA1975.pdf)). Brown's field test found naive averages worst in every case ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0803.3697)). Franks et al.'s stability, discrimination and independence meta-metrics give a ready-made framework for screening metrics ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bpj/jqsprt/v12y2016i4p151-165n3.html)). For inference, use Freedman–Lane residual permutation for partial effects ([UVM notes](https://www.uvm.edu/~statdhtx/StatPages/R/RandomizationTestsWithR/permut2.pdf)), Benjamini–Hochberg FDR across the position × metric grid ([PDF](https://www.dcscience.net/Benjamini-Hochberg-1995-FDR.pdf)), lme4-style varying slopes by position group ([JSS](https://www.jstatsoft.org/v67/i01)), and Heckman or hurdle models for snap-dependent outcomes ([Econometric Society](https://www.econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/1979/01/01/sample-selection-bias-specification-error)). Speed curves can be summarised either by FPCA ([Ramsay & Silverman](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/b98888)) or by the 2–3 parameter sprint model. At this sample size the parametric route has the better interpretability-to-degrees-of-freedom ratio.

The public "Measure the Athlete" entry reports that **0 of 144 position-drill → NFL tests survived** family-wise permutation ([bansal1600](https://github.com/bansal1600/kaggle-NFL-big-data-2027)). That is consistent with this power arithmetic. New entries should not run more drill × position tests. They should reduce dimensionality with physics, pool across positions hierarchically, and pre-register one primary outcome per position family.

## Five original analyses that stay clear of COD-stability ground

None of the ideas below asks whether Combine COD is stable relative to in-game COD. That is the existing repo's territory. Each idea instead uses a physical model or a causal/selection framing that the existing repo does not use.

### Idea 1: the capacity-utilisation ratio

**Approach.** Fit the time-shifted mono-exponential model to each rookie's 10 Hz 40-yard trace to get vmax, τ and amax, plus F0 and Pmax using body mass. Separately, build Morin's in-situ A–S profile from the rookie's game frames. Use signed tangential acceleration, 0.2 m/s bins, the top values per bin, and DBSCAN outlier removal. Define **utilisation = S0_game / vmax_combine** and **A0_game / amax_combine**.

**What it can show.** Which positions and which rookies express a lower or higher share of their tested capacity. Whether low utilisation, after shrinkage, relates to EB-shrunk NGS outcomes (separation_at_pass_forward, player_get_off), beyond the raw Combine numbers.

**Literature anchors and checks.** Clark 2019 and the 2025 JSCR paper anchor the Combine side. Morin 2021 and Clavel 2023 anchor the game side, including the 20–95% speed coverage rule. Flag players whose game data lack coverage. The A–S intercepts and the vmax fit cross-validate each other on the 40 as a data-quality check.

**Why it is new.** The repo compares top speed across contexts. This idea compares *model-based force-velocity capacity* with an *in-situ frontier*. That has not been done in American football.

### Idea 2: drill-phase decomposition with a tracking COD deficit

**Approach.** Segment the 5-10-5 and 3-cone traces into acceleration, braking, turn and re-acceleration. Braking onset is the first frame of tangential acceleration below −2.5 m/s², Harper's threshold. Compute a per-turn COD deficit against the athlete's *own* fitted x(t) from the 40, which removes the need for a separate 10 m test (Nimphius 2016; Lockie 2017). Fit each athlete's **entry-speed versus turn-angle trade-off** across the 180° shuttle turns and the 90° 3-cone turns (Dos'Santos 2018).

**What it can show.** Which phase carries the between-athlete variance in drill time, and whether the deficit adds anything once a latent speed factor is controlled. Use SEM (Westfall–Yarkoni) instead of a naive partial correlation.

**Why it is new.** This is a within-drill mechanistic decomposition and an incremental-validity test. It does not compare drill stability with game stability. Run the AUT-style reliability audit on the phases first, and report phases that fail it as unreliable instead of using them.

### Idea 3: a Combine-physics ghost of each rookie

**Approach.** Use each rookie's Combine-fitted capacity (vmax, τ, braking capacity from the drills, and a centripetal ceiling from the curved 3-cone segments, a_c = v·|dθ/dt|) to simulate a **speed-capped reachable region** over 0.5 s and 1.0 s from every in-game state. In effect, this is a physics "ghost" of the rookie, borrowing the 2026 Ghostbusters framing. Compare it with Brefeld-style empirical displacement quantiles from the rookie's actual snaps. The residual, empirical area minus ghost area, measures how much more or less ground the player covers than his Combine physics predicts.

**What it can show.** Whether ghost residuals at route breaks (conditioned on Chu route clusters) or in DB backpedal states relate to separation outcomes.

**Why it is new.** It answers the Vienna paper's call for capped, validated motion models. It uses the Combine as a *generative prior*, not as a correlate.

### Idea 4: get-off physics for the trenches

**Approach.** Linemen are where the Combine literature is weakest (McGee & Burkett 2003). For rookie EDGE and DT players, fit the mono-exponential model to the first 1.0–1.5 s after the snap. Compare game τ and amax with the 10-yd-split portion of the Combine 40 fit. The RB 10-yd-split finding (Teramoto 2016) suggests early acceleration is the most transferable component. Add a **STRAIN-like closing rate** toward a fixed pocket point 7 yards behind the line of scrimmage.

**Validation and outcomes.** Validate against NGS player_get_off, then relate the measures to time_to_pressure using a multilevel model with player random effects, as STRAIN does. For rookie OL, use the mirror image: pass-set retreat speed and braking against pressure_allowed.

**Why it is new.** It replaces a 40-yard dash that linemen never run in games with the 1-second sprint they run on every snap.

### Idea 5: market-adjusted athleticism with selection correction

**Question.** Does tracking-derived athleticism (Ideas 1–2 parameters, summarised as a latent factor) predict rookie per-snap NGS outcomes *beyond draft capital*?

**Design.** Use nested models: mass + position + 40, then adding log pick, then adding the tracking factor. Evaluate them with leave-one-draft-class-out cross-validation, a stratified player bootstrap, and Freedman–Lane permutation within position. Handle selection explicitly with a hurdle model: P(reaches N snaps), then efficiency given that. Add inverse-probability weights, and run a Keefer-style comparison at the round boundaries. Compare the results with the reported NGS prospect scores, because the 2027 data reportedly include them for 510 prospects ([i-programmer](https://www.i-programmer.info/news/204-challenges/19221-nfl-big-data-bowl-2027-competition-now-open.html)).

**Why it is new.** It is the first explicit selection-corrected, market-adjusted validity test of Combine tracking. The literature review found none. It also directly checks NGS's unpublished claims.

## Conclusion

The most useful shift these sources support is to stop treating Combine drills as predictors and treat them as **calibration runs for a physical model of each athlete**. Sprint science supplies a parametric model with acceptable reliability and an in-game estimator for the same parameters. Deceleration science says which phase of a drill to trust. Soccer motion models show how to turn capacity into reachable space. A writeup built this way can report interpretable effect sizes: "rookie X expresses 88% of his tested acceleration capacity". This avoids repeating the existing repo's null grid of drill-outcome correlations. The statistical literature is equally clear on what makes such claims credible: reliability before validity, latent rather than residualised speed controls, and explicit handling of the draft-capital selection that Keefer quantified.

Three uncertainties should be stated in any submission. First, the 2027 schema, including whether `a` is signed and how the vendor smooths speed, was not read. Second, no peer-reviewed NFL in-situ A–S or tracking-based drill decomposition exists to benchmark against. Third, several key citations (Clark 2019 co-authors, the 2025 JSCR authors, Teramoto pages, the BDB Figure-8 paper) need full-text confirmation before publication.
