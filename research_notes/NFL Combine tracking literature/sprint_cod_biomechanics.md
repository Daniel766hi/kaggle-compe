## Q1. Mono-exponential speed-time model and force-velocity profiling (Samozino, Morin, Clark; NFL Combine applications)

### Takeaway
The mono-exponential model v(t) = vmax·(1 − e^(−t/τ)) is standard. Samozino et al. (2016) turned it into a field method for horizontal force-velocity-power (FVP) profiling. Clark et al. (JSCR 2019) and a 2025 JSCR paper (n = 2,156) fitted it to NFL Combine 40-yd split times. With 10 Hz tracking, the curve can be fitted directly to the speed trace, so it no longer has to be inferred from 2 to 3 split times.

### Cited Findings
- **Samozino P, Rabita G, Dorel S, Slawinski J, Peyrot N, Saez de Villarreal E, Morin JB. "A simple method for measuring power, force, velocity properties, and mechanical effectiveness in sprint running." Scand J Med Sci Sports 2016;26(6):648–658. DOI 10.1111/sms.12490.** Online in 2015, in the 2016 issue. The full author list is from memory **[author list partly UNVERIFIED; title/journal/DOI verified]**. — [HAL record](https://hal.univ-reunion.fr/hal-01389134/document); [UPO record](https://investiga.upo.es/documentos/5ec78fe72999520a1d5580cc); [Scinapse](https://scinapse.io/papers/1736647735)
  - The method is "an inverse dynamic approach applied to the body center of mass, estimating step-averaged ground reaction forces from only anthropometric and spatiotemporal data". — [UPO record](https://investiga.upo.es/documentos/5ec78fe72999520a1d5580cc)
  - Validated against force plates in 9 sprinters. The abstract reports low bias and narrow limits of agreement for F0, V0, Pmax, the FV slope and mechanical effectiveness (RF/DRF), and between-trial SEM "<5%". — [UPO record](https://investiga.upo.es/documentos/5ec78fe72999520a1d5580cc)
  - A 2016 iPhone-app study (Romero-Franco et al.) applied the model and compared it with photocells and radar. — [digitum.um.es PDF](https://digitum.um.es/digitum/bitstream/10201/149780/1/2016_Romero-Franco%20N_Sprint%20performance%20and%20mechanical%20outputs%20computed%20with%20an%20iPhone%20app%20-%20comparison%20with%20existing%20reference%20methods.pdf)
  - Standard formulation (check against the paper): v(t)=vmax(1−e^(−t/τ)); a(t)=(vmax/τ)e^(−t/τ); x(t)=vmax(t+τe^(−t/τ))−vmax·τ; F_H(t)=m·a(t)+F_aero (F_aero≈k·(v−v_wind)²); P=F_H·v. F0 and V0 come from a linear F–v regression, Pmax=F0·V0/4, RF=F_H/F_resultant, and DRF is the slope of RF against v.
- **Model history and parameterisation:** The model was "first proposed by Furusawa, Hill & Parkinson (1927) and popularized by Clark et al. (2017) and Samozino et al. (2016)". Its parameters are MSS (maximum sprinting speed) and TAU, "the time required to reach a velocity equal to 63.2% of MSS". Maximum acceleration is MAC = MSS/TAU. The source is a Jovanović preprint on SportRxiv (not peer reviewed). — [SportRxiv preprint](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/download/179/315)
  - The {shorts} R package (Jovanović & Vescovi, 2022) implements these models and handles timing-gate problems such as flying starts and time shifts. — [LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/dlv/Record/4076968)
- **"The NFL combine 40-yard dash: How important is maximum velocity?" J Strength Cond Res, 2019.** The lead author is Ken P. Clark per a secondary source. Co-authors (Rieger, Bruno, Stearne) and volume/issue (33(6):1542–1550) are **[UNVERIFIED]**. — [LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4054398?lng=de); [Substack attribution to Clark](https://joeeisenmann.substack.com/p/fastfriday-nfl-combine-special-edition)
  - Data: 2016 NFL Combine, public 9.1 m and 18.3 m splits plus 36.6 m time, fitted with a mono-exponential model.
  - Main result: "Maximum velocity was strongly correlated with sprint performance across 9.1, 18.3, and 36.6 m (r² of 0.72, 0.83, and 0.94, respectively)."
  - Fast and slow groups accelerated with a similar pattern relative to vmax. τ was reported as about 0.77 s for both groups; this comes from a search snippet of the record. — [LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4054398?lng=de)
  - Conclusion: "more maximum velocity training may be warranted for athletes preparing for the NFL Combine."
  - Interpretation of τ in the NFL context, from a practitioner source: a small τ (for example 0.6) means the runner reaches a high fraction of vmax quickly; a large τ (for example 1.0) means it takes longer. — [1080 Motion](https://www.1080motion.com/news/the-nfl-combine-sprint-profiles-and-the-importance-of-overspeed-training)
- **"Position-specific differences in speed profiles among National Football League Scouting Combine participants." J Strength Cond Res, 2025.** n = 2,156 Combine players. vmax, τ (snippet shows "t") and amax were derived from the 40-yd dash and its 10- and 20-yd splits. Skill positions (DB, WR) "had the greatest vmax and amax, and lowest t, while OL [had] the lowest vmax and amax, and highest t". Big skill positions came next, then DL. **[Authors UNVERIFIED]** — [LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4089847?lng=en); [SPONET record](https://www.sponet.de/sponet/Record/4091657?lng=en)
- **"Sprint acceleration mechanical profiling for the NFL draft." Sport Performance & Science Reports, Issue 27 (2018), pp. 1–3.** It describes the FVP profile of Combine athletes and asks which mechanical variables relate to draft selection. **[Authors UNVERIFIED; results not retrieved]** — [IAT record](https://www.iat.uni-leipzig.de/datenbanken/iks/drv-rugby/Record/4048089); article URL from the record: https://sportperfsci.com/sprint-acceleration-mechanical-profiling-for-the-nfl-draft/
- Critique: "football players rarely run a 40-yard dash during competition nor do they reach maximal velocity." — [1080 Motion: Vmax for short sprint performance at NFL Combine](https://1080motion.com/vmax-short-sprint-performance/)

### Inferences
- How to compute this from 10 Hz tracking. Take the speed trace s(t) from the 40 yd start (motion onset is the first frame where s exceeds a small threshold, for example 0.2–0.5 m/s, or a jump in displacement). Fit vmax and τ by nonlinear least squares, adding a time-shift parameter t0 for reaction or onset uncertainty, as {shorts} does. Alternatively, fit x(t) to the cumulative distance, which is less sensitive to noise than differentiated speed. Derived features: vmax, τ, amax = vmax/τ, F0 = m·amax (plus a small aero term), Pmax ≈ m·vmax²/(4τ) when aero is ignored, the fit RMSE, and the residual pattern (for example a late deceleration before 40 yd, or a non-exponential start).
- At 10 Hz a 4.4 s dash gives about 44 samples. That is far more than the 3 splits used by Clark 2019 and the 2025 JSCR paper, so the τ estimate should be more stable. The tracking speed field may be smoothed by the vendor, which can bias τ upward. This is an assumption to test, for example by comparing fits on speed with fits on position.
- Clark 2019 found that τ was similar across speed groups and that vmax explained most of the variance. Two consequences follow: at fixed body mass, vmax will dominate 40 time, and τ and amax add information mainly through between-position differences (2025 JSCR).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper was found that fits the mono-exponential model to Combine **tracking** data (as opposed to split times).
- The co-authors of Clark 2019, the authors of the 2025 JSCR paper and the authors of SPSR 2018 could not be confirmed.
- The exact equations in Samozino 2016 (aerodynamic constant, body-height formula) were not read in full text.

## Q2. In-situ acceleration–speed (A–S) profiles from GPS/tracking

### Takeaway
Morin et al. (J Biomech 2021) showed that a linear A–S profile can be built from the maximal accelerations observed at each speed in routine GPS data. It gives A0 (intercept) and S0 (x-intercept) without a dedicated test. Follow-ups in youth soccer (2023), rugby union (2024) and LaLiga support its reliability, provided the data span roughly 20–95% of max speed.

### Cited Findings
- **Morin JB, Le Mat Y, Osgnach C, et al. "Individual acceleration-speed profile in-situ: A proof of concept in professional football players." J Biomech 2021;123:110524.** The volume and article number come from the reference list of a citing paper. The DOI 10.1016/j.jbiomech.2021.110524 follows the publisher's pattern but was not shown on a record page **[DOI UNVERIFIED]**. — [LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4070585?lng=en); citing reference list: [Applied Sciences 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/12/24/12987)
  - Method, per a search summary of the record: GPS speed data from 16 professional male players across training sessions. For each 0.2 m/s speed bin from 3 m/s up to the player's top speed, the maximal acceleration was kept, and a linear regression was fitted. Profiles were "highly linear (all R² > 0.984)" and reliable when determined 2 weeks apart. The bin details came via a search summary, so check them against the full text. — [LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4070585?lng=en)
  - A0 is the "theoretical maximal acceleration (y-intercept of the AS linear relationship)" and S0 the "theoretical maximum speed (x-intercept)". — quoted in a later review: [JHK LaLiga study](https://jhk.termedia.pl/The-Acceleration-Speed-Profile-in-Professional-Soccer-Players-nA-Comparative-Study,220252,0,2.html)
- **"Reliability of individual acceleration-speed profile in-situ in elite youth soccer players." J Biomech 2023;153:111602. DOI 10.1016/j.jbiomech.2023.111602.** Authors per a search summary: Clavel, Leduc, Morin, Buchheit, Lacome **[authors partly UNVERIFIED]**. 18 elite U19 players over 3 training weeks. A0, S0 and the AS slope had low week-to-week variability. Reliability depended more on the spread of data points than on the number of sessions, and "practitioners must make sure that the values cover a sufficient range of raw data [20-95% of maximum speed]". — [LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/dfb/Record/4081845?lng=en); DOI from a citing reference list: [Crossref listing for Sensors 2024](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3390%2FS24196192)
- **"Individual In-Situ GPS-Derived Acceleration-Speed Profiling: Toward Automatization and Refinement in Male Professional Rugby Union Players." Sports Medicine – Open, Jan 2024 (PMC10784250; DOI 10.1186/s40798-023-00672-7 per the springeropen URL).** 49 professional rugby union players. A density-based clustering algorithm flags outlier points before the fit. — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10784250/); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/d97f3324cc1b4dbfa34e9affffa23c0b); [SpringerOpen table](https://sportsmedicine-open.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40798-023-00672-7/tables/1)
- **"Comparison of acceleration-speed profiles from training and competition to individual maximal sprint efforts."** This is a validity comparison of in-situ A–S profiles against dedicated sprints. **[Details/results UNVERIFIED]** — [LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/dfb/Record/4083731)
- Clavel, Leduc and Morin (IJSPP 2022) measured the validity and reliability of sprint force-velocity profiles from GPS devices in elite athletes. This is cited in a 2025 IJSM reference list. — [Thieme IJSM 2025](https://www.thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/html/10.1055/a-2655-0650)
- Cardoso et al., J Sports Sci 2023, cross-sectional study of an elite academy: U14 players had significantly lower S0 than older groups, with no difference in A0. — [LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4087077)
- 2025 study of 136 academy players: accumulated sprint distance was associated with higher S0 in several age groups, and "no significant correlation was found between any external load metric and A0". — [LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4094673?lng=de)
- "Acceleration speed profiles in LaLiga: the influence of initial running speed and differences between positional roles." Biology of Sport. — [Termedia](https://www.termedia.pl/Acceleration-speed-profiles-in-LaLiga-the-influence-of-initial-r-nrunning-speed-and-differences-between-positional-roles-in-elite-r-nfootball-players,78,56014,0,1.html)
- An A–S profile was also computed in curvilinear sprints with radii of 6.15–12.15 m in youth soccer: "ASParea and S0 decreased at tighter radii", and radius "mainly affect late acceleration and top speed performance". — [Termedia, Biology of Sport](https://www.termedia.pl/Does-curvilinear-sprint-training-with-different-radii-improve-r-nmultidirectional-speed-and-sprint-mechanics-in-youth-soccer-r-nplayers-,78,57300,0,0.html)

### Inferences
- How to compute this from 10 Hz tracking. Pool a player's frames across all Combine drills (40, shuttle, 3-cone, position drills). Bin speed in 0.2 m/s steps from about 3 m/s to the player's max. Keep the top one or two accelerations per bin, remove outliers (95% prediction interval or DBSCAN as in the rugby paper), and regress a on s. This gives A0, S0 and the slope. Only use the tangential (signed) acceleration component, not the vendor's total acceleration magnitude, if that field is unsigned. The tangential component is a_t = (v_{t+1}−v_{t−1})/(2Δt), or the acceleration projected onto the unit velocity vector.
- Combine data are a few maximal efforts, not weeks of training. Coverage of 20–95% of max speed is likely met for the 40, but the shuttle and 3-cone only reach moderate speeds. This works in favour of A0 estimation and against S0 estimation outside the 40.
- In-situ A–S S0 and the vmax from the mono-exponential fit should agree closely for the 40. Their difference is a possible check on data quality.

### Gaps
- No full-text check of the Morin 2021 numbers. The reliability statistics (CV/ICC values) for 2021 and 2023 were not retrieved verbatim.
- No A–S profiling study in American football was found.

## Q3. COD deficit, COD speed vs agility, deceleration demands, angle–velocity trade-off, curvilinear limits

### Takeaway
Total COD test time is heavily confounded by linear speed. The COD deficit (COD time minus linear sprint time over the same distance) isolates turning ability better. The angle–velocity trade-off and the deceleration literature say that entry speed, braking capacity and turn angle jointly determine COD time. All of these are computable from 10 Hz x, y and speed.

### Cited Findings
- **Nimphius S, Callaghan SJ, Spiteri T, Lockie RG. "Change of direction deficit: A more isolated measure of change of direction performance than total 505 time." J Strength Cond Res 2016;30(11):3024–3032.** — [ECU repository](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworkspost2013/2635); [BISp](https://www.bisp-surf.de/Record/PU201611007652); [Scinapse](https://scinapse.io/papers/2301686871)
  - COD deficit = 505 time − 10 m sprint time. In 17 cricketers the deficit was closely related to 505 time but showed essentially no relationship with sprint time, whereas 505 time did correlate with sprint time. 5 of 17 athletes were ranked differently depending on which measure was used. This summary comes from abstract snippets.
- **Lockie RG et al. "A Methodological Report: Adapting the 505 Change-of-Direction Speed Test Specific to American Football." J Strength Cond Res 2017 (PMID 27227795).** 25 high-school football players. The A505 uses a 10-yd sprint plus a 180° turn over 5 yd. COD deficit = A505 time − 0–10 yd time. Reliability: "no between-session differences for the A505 (p = 0.45-0.76; intraclass correlation coefficient = 0.84-0.95; TE = 2.03-4.13%)". The A505 correlated with the 505 and the 40-yd sprint (r = 0.58–0.92). R/DB were faster than linemen in the A505 for both legs and in the right-leg COD deficit. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27227795/)
- "'Change of direction deficit' measurement in Division I American football players" (ECU). **[Details UNVERIFIED]** — [ECU record](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworks2013/287)
- **Sheppard JM, Young WB. "Agility literature review: classifications, training and testing." J Sports Sci 2006;24(9):919–932. DOI 10.1080/02640410500457109.** It proposes agility as "a rapid whole-body movement with change of velocity or direction in response to a stimulus". Pre-planned tests (such as the 5-10-5 and 3-cone) measure COD speed, not agility. — [BISp](https://www.bisp-surf.de/Record/PU201401000015)
- **Dos'Santos T, Thomas C, Comfort P, Jones PA. "The effect of angle and velocity on change of direction biomechanics: an angle-velocity trade-off." Sports Med 2018;48(10):2235–2253. DOI 10.1007/s40279-018-0968-3.** "faster approaches compromise the execution of the intended COD", and this is influenced by physical capacity. Sharper and faster cuts increase knee loading (a "performance-injury conflict"). — [Salford repository](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1378901/the-effect-of-angle-and-velocity-on-change-of-direction-biomechanics-an-angle-velocity-trade-off); [SpringerMedicine](https://www.springermedicine.com/the-effect-of-angle-and-velocity-on-change-of-direction-biomecha/22255642)
  - Paraphrase in a later paper: "faster approaching velocities limit the subsequent COD due to greater braking required to change the momentum from higher velocities, while the angle of the COD sets limitations of the approaching velocity". — [Frontiers Sports Act Living 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2020.594567/pdf)
- **Harper DJ, Carling C, Kiely J. "High-Intensity Acceleration and Deceleration Demands in Elite Team Sports Competitive Match Play: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Observational Studies." Sports Med 2019;49:1923–1947. DOI 10.1007/s40279-019-01170-1.** Thresholds used: high >2.5 m·s⁻², very high >3.5 m·s⁻². Pooled effect sizes were not retrieved. — [PMC6851047](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6851047/); [CLoK](https://clok.uclan.ac.uk/29510)
- **Harper DJ, McBurnie AJ, Dos'Santos T, et al. "Biomechanical and Neuromuscular Performance Requirements of Horizontal Deceleration: A Review with Implications for Random Intermittent Multi-Directional Sports." Sports Med 2022;52(10):2321–2354. DOI 10.1007/s40279-022-01693-0.** Deceleration peak forces occur early in stance and are "up to 2.7 times greater than those seen during the first steps of a maximal horizontal acceleration". The key qualities are braking force control and braking force attenuation. — [PMC9474351](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9474351/); [UL Pure](https://pure.ul.ie/en/publications/biomechanical-and-neuromuscular-performance-requirements-of-horiz/)
  - One secondary summary claims decelerations impose "~41% greater mechanical load per meter" than accelerations. This is from a summary site and not verified in the paper **[UNVERIFIED]**. — [Physio-network](https://www.physio-network.com/research-reviews/other/biomechanical-and-neuromuscular-performance-requirements-of-horizontal-deceleration-a-review-with-implications-for-random-intermittent-multi%E2%80%91directional-sports)
- **Curvilinear sprinting:**
  - About 85% of maximum-velocity actions in elite teams are curvilinear. — [PMC7179881](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7179881)
  - Inertial and LPS systems estimate centripetal force from turn radius and speed. A 2020 study assessed GPS and UWB accuracy for this. — [PMC7179881](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7179881)
  - LPS-measured curvilinear sprint (3-point-line arc) in basketball, with a reliability study of max/mean acceleration, velocity and centripetal force. — [LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4084254?lng=de)
  - Radius effects on S0 and A–S: see Q2. No paper was found that gives a sport-specific centripetal-acceleration ceiling.

### Inferences
- **COD deficit from tracking.** For the 5-10-5 and 3-cone, compute time over each straight segment and compare it with the time the same athlete needs to cover that distance in the 40, taken from the fitted x(t) of the mono-exponential model. The difference is a per-turn tracking COD deficit, which needs no separate 10 m sprint.
- **Angle–velocity.** Turn angle is the change in heading between the entry and exit segments (from dir or atan2 of dx, dy). Entry speed is the speed at the onset of braking (the first frame where tangential acceleration goes below a threshold, for example −2.5 m/s², using Harper's threshold). Analyse the relation of entry speed to angle across 90° turns (3-cone) and 180° turns (shuttle).
- **Deceleration metrics.** Peak and mean negative tangential acceleration, braking distance (from braking onset to v_min), braking time, v_min at the turn, and time near zero speed. Then re-acceleration τ and amax from a mono-exponential fit to the exit speed.
- **Curvilinear and centripetal.** a_c = v²/r = v·|dθ/dt|, where θ is heading. Compute the heading rate from dir, or from the cross product of the velocity and acceleration vectors (|v×a|/|v|). For the 3-cone loop and the gauntlet or figure-8, peak a_c and the speed held at a given curvature describe curving ability. At 10 Hz, dθ/dt needs smoothing, for example Savitzky–Golay over 5–7 frames. This is an assumption, not taken from the literature.

### Gaps
- No exact quantitative angle–speed relationships from Dos'Santos 2018 (narrative review).
- The pooled meta-analytic values in Harper 2019 were not retrieved.
- No centripetal ceiling value was found in the literature.

## Q4. Reliability of the 5-10-5 and 3-cone tests, what drives time, and tracking-based drill decomposition

### Takeaway
The pro-agility shuttle is reliable as a whole (TE about 2%). One AUT thesis segmented it into acceleration, deceleration, COD and re-acceleration phases and found good timing-light phase reliability but poor radar reliability. No peer-reviewed paper was found that decomposes NFL Combine drill time using tracking sensors. The closest league work is the Big Data Bowl paper that led to the Figure-8 drill (2020 Combine).

### Cited Findings
- Pro-agility (5-10-5), whole-test reliability: test-retest r = 0.88–0.95 and typical percentage error 1.95–2.40%. This is quoted on Physiopedia; the primary study was not identified **[primary UNVERIFIED]**. — [Physiopedia](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Pro-Agility_%285-10-5%29_Test); [Science for Sport](https://www.scienceforsport.com/pro-agility-5-10-5-test/)
- AUT thesis (Auckland University of Technology) segmenting the pro-agility shuttle into acceleration, deceleration, COD and re-acceleration phases. Timing-light phases had "CV = 0.95 to 4.42%" and "ICC 0.90 to 0.99" between sessions 2 and 3. Radar velocity profiling was less consistent, with "ICC = 0.14 to 0.76". **[Author/title UNVERIFIED]** — [AUT repository](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/items/7fa1f785-f30c-4202-ad37-eae757cf0a6c)
- Critique of the pro-agility test as a COD measure: the influence of the sprint distances on its ability to measure COD speed. — [Science for Sport](https://www.scienceforsport.com/pro-agility-5-10-5-test/)
- The NFL Football Operations article reports that a 2019 Big Data Bowl winning paper, using Next Gen Stats tracking, led to the **Figure 8 drill**, which debuted at the 2020 Combine. The article could not be fetched, so the paper's authors and metric are **[UNVERIFIED]**. — [NFL Operations](https://operations.nfl.com/gameday/analytics/stats-articles/big-data-bowl-winning-paper-leads-to-new-drill-at-the-nfl-combine)
- At MIT Sloan 2015, NFL staff said in-game tracking might change which speed measures matter, for example 60-yd speed for WR/DB against 20-yd speed for OL. This is a panel remark, not a paper. — [ABC7/ESPN](https://abc7news.com/post/nfl-to-study-data-from-combine-drills/537257/)
- An informal student analysis (about 100 rookie WRs) found that 3-cone time had near-zero R² with rookie production. It is not peer reviewed. — [Berkeley Sports Analytics](https://sportsanalytics.studentorg.berkeley.edu/articles/speed-kills.html)

### Inferences
- **Proposed segmentation at 10 Hz.**
  - Turns: local speed minima within known cone geometry.
  - Phases, labelled by the sign and magnitude of tangential acceleration: acceleration (a_t > 0), steady, braking (a_t below −threshold), turn (speed below about 1–2 m/s or a large heading change), and re-acceleration.
  - Each phase's share of total drill time shows which component (entry speed, deceleration, re-acceleration) drives the result. Expected from the angle–velocity trade-off: for 180° turns, braking and re-acceleration dominate; for the 3-cone's 90° and curvilinear segments, centripetal capacity matters.
- The AUT result is a warning: radar (and by analogy 10 Hz positional speed) gives less reliable within-phase velocity metrics than gate times. Phase metrics from tracking should be checked for reliability before use, for example by comparing across an athlete's two trials.
- The 3-cone (L-drill) has no primary reliability study in these notes **[gap]**.

### Gaps
- No primary peer-reviewed reliability study for the 3-cone/L-drill was located within the tool budget.
- No peer-reviewed or Sloan paper was found that uses tracking to decompose Combine drill times. The Big Data Bowl Figure-8 paper details are unverified.
- No literature was found on backpedal-to-sprint transition (hip turn) mechanics or pass-pro mirror kinematics. The general COD and deceleration metrics above would be the closest analogue.
